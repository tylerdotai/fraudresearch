# Prior-publication search

## Search plan

Use the exact legal/DBA names and stable identifiers separately, then combine with conduct/program/project/period terms. Search official agency audit/OIG/enforcement pages and court materials where publicly authorized. Use journalism to find originals, not replace originals. Record unavailable archives and paywalls; do not bypass them.

A search log records query, source class, date/time, returned relevant URLs, actual retrieval status, exact supported passage, source event/publication date and coverage limitation. Distinguish publication date, amended-document date and retrieval date. Preserve corrected/retracted reporting and case dispositions.

## Claim-by-claim delta

Do not label the whole report original because one small calculation is new. Map each proposed allegation to:
- What the prior source already states.
- What independent evidence was collected.
- What transformation/join/calculation was performed.
- What new supported inference follows.
- Why alternative explanations do not resolve it, or what remains unresolved.

Describe independence by provenance, not URL count. An OIG release, newspaper summary and social post quoting that release may all share one upstream allegation.

## Synthetic example

A public audit states a payment was excessive. An analyst rediscovers it and adds a graph; no new alleged conduct is established. If a separate reproducible analysis later identifies a documented cross-entity transaction pattern not stated in the audit, assess that specific delta. Do not imply it is unknown to investigators merely because public searches did not find it.

## Stop or handoff

If the current material is a restatement, record that outcome and either stop or identify the precise new evidence required. A private report may still be useful without reward eligibility. Do not race to submit an incomplete tip to beat another hypothetical tipster. Submission is a separately authorized action governed by current procedures.
