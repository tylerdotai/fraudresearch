# Mechanism-to-evidence tests

| Hypothesis | Evidence needed | Legitimate alternatives to test | Unsupported shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Billing for services not delivered | Actual claims/payment plus reliable service evidence for matched dates | Corrections, consolidated billing, subcontract/branch delivery, timing | Registry or address alone |
| Ineligible enrollment or licensure | Correct jurisdiction/program rules and dated enrollment/license records | Different license types, renewal lag, exemptions, branches | NPI issuance treated as licence |
| Duplicate payments | Claim/invoice/payment IDs, reversals and dates | Resubmissions, adjustments, split payments, repeated publication | Duplicate CCN or identical amount |
| Concealed related-party arrangement | Dated ownership/control and transaction terms | Common registered agent, service address, unrelated tenants | Same address means common beneficial owner |
| Unusual utilization or quality | Matched cohort, denominator, definitions, period, suppression | Patient mix, agency size, changes in measures, small samples | Outlier means fraud |
| Clinical eligibility manipulation | Applicable clinical rules and authoritative evidence | Recertification or permissible treatment/course | Long survival or diagnosis pattern alone |

The last row is a research evidence requirement, not a request to collect private patient records. Without lawful appropriate evidence, report the observable public facts and stop the clinical inference.

## Synthetic example: relative spending

A synthetic agency has a spending-comparison measure greater than the national reference. This supports a relative-measure observation only after validating the release definition, cohort and footnotes. It does not reveal dollars lost. Investigate case mix, episode definitions, denominator and period before suggesting a mechanism. Do not turn a ratio into a payment total.

## Synthetic example: address cluster

Several synthetic organizations share a mailing suite. Check address purpose, offices versus mailing services, property/tenant records, organization history and dated ownership documents. A repeated suite can support a candidate cluster. A factual ownership edge requires independent ownership evidence; even common ownership does not establish unlawful billing.

## Findings matrix

For each observation record: mechanism; source ID and locator; actual counting unit; period; proposed inference; required missing source; strongest benign explanation; evidence sought that would falsify the inference; result; claim classification. Preserve negative results. Use neutral labels such as `identity unresolved`, `source duplicate`, `measure not comparable` or `requires payment evidence`, rather than a fraud score.

## Stop conditions

Stop or narrow when the necessary layer is unavailable, the crosswalk is unsupported, dates do not overlap, the purported amount is not money, or a benign explanation resolves the observation. A legally/clinically nuanced conclusion requires the relevant authoritative rules and appropriate expertise; a software check cannot supply either.
